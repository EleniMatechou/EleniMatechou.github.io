On 6 October 2026 I took part in a workshop on soil microbiome and biodiversity in Inverness, organised by Cristian Navarro Waggershauser of [Inverness College UHI](https://www.inverness.uhi.ac.uk/). The workshop brought together researchers and practitioners from the [James Hutton Institute](https://www.hutton.ac.uk/), [UKCEH](https://www.ceh.ac.uk/), the [University of Edinburgh](https://www.ed.ac.uk/), [BioSS](https://www.bioss.ac.uk/), [Forestry and Land Scotland](https://forestryandland.gov.scot/), [NatureScot](https://www.nature.scot/)'s Peatland ACTION programme and the [Cairngorms National Park Authority](https://cairngorms.co.uk/).

![Workshop participants](/images/soil-workshop-2026.jpg)

One question raised in the discussion was whether soil microbiome research should change what organisations delivering restoration on the ground do, for example in peatland rewetting, reducing deer densities and species reintroductions. The ecological questions we discussed included whether functional recovery of soil communities matches or lags behind compositional recovery, patterns and drivers of diversity across latitude, elevation and habitat gradients, and the relationship between above- and below-ground communities. On the methods side, we discussed optimal spatial and temporal sampling designs across broad taxonomic groups, and marker cocktails that could lower laboratory costs and make molecular tools easier to use in routine monitoring.
