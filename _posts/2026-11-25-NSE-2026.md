On 17 March 2026 I presented our work on mathematics and AI in ecology at Queen Mary's [Night of Science and Engineering 2026](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/nse/2026/), the fourth annual showcase reception of the Faculty of Science and Engineering. This year's theme was 'Local Impact - Global Reach', and the event brought together partners from around 200 organisations across industry, government, research funding and the third sector. The [programme](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/nse/2026/programme/) included speeches by Colin Bailey and Wen Wang, a talk by Elizabeth Gardner, Deputy Director of Science at Kew Gardens, honorary degrees for Yongyuth Yuthavong and David Owens, and presentations by three Queen Mary PhD students. There is more on the evening in the Faculty's [news story](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/news/latest-news/2026/science-and-engineering/se/queen-marys-night-of-science-and-engineering-celebrates-innovation-partnership-and-impact.html).

Our stand, [Mathematics and AI in Ecology](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/nse/2026/aiconservation/), was a joint effort with Silvia Liverani, Kostas Papafitsoros and Linus Wunderlich from the [Centre for Probability, Statistics and Data Science](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/cpsd/). It covered our research at the intersection of ecology, statistics and AI, from AI-based identification of individual animals in photographs to statistical models for citizen science data.

![From pixels to populations: using mathematics and AI to study wildlife](/images/nse-2026-turtle.png)

At the stand, guests could play our game, [From Pixels to Populations](https://www.frompixelstopopulations.com/). The game follows the full research pipeline with real field data: players capture photographs, use AI to recognise individual sea turtles, and apply statistical ecology models to estimate population size. Along the way they work with probability, uncertainty and bias. We developed it in partnership with [Deep-C Vision](https://www.deepcvision.co.uk/), with funding from the [EPSRC](https://www.ukri.org/councils/epsrc/) Additional Skills Funding Scheme.

The game is freely available to schools, educators and the public, and has since been used in a workshop for 36 students from two schools. More details are on the [project page](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/cpsd/impact/frompixelstopopulations/) and in the School's [news item on the launch](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/maths/news-and-events/news-/items/from-pixels-to-populations-researchers-launch-an-interactive-educational-platform-showcasing-maths-ai-and-ecology-in-action.html).
